# What a write a model proposed records

Status: draft, 2026-11-13, for jayostis/cascade-runtime-js#758. Adds `cascade:ModelProposerRole`, the shapes
`cascade:ModelProposedActShape`, `cascade:ModelProposerAssociationShape` and `cascade:ModelProposedJudgmentShape`, and
the rule Y3 of `runtime/consent.feature`, with Y2's examples. Amends `cascade:PersonJudgmentShape`, which allowed an
act one association, and `judgments.md`, row "maker". Depends on `consent-tiers.md` (a write to a pod is at ask) and
`judgments.md`.

## Question

A model offered a person's pods may propose a write: an entry, or a judgment (same, different, wrong, before), or the
retraction of one. The door asks the person, and writes only on their yes (`consent-tiers.md`, row "ask"). What does
the pod record of it, so that a reader can tell a write a model proposed from one the person made directly, and see
who said yes?

What makes it hard: a pod only grows (`X1`), so what it records of a write is all a reader will ever know of how it
came to be; the person's judgments already name their author in a role (`judgments.md`, row "maker"), and the views
treat a person's judgment differently from the matcher's, so a confirmed proposal must stay the person's; and the
door knows little of the model: an MCP server is told the client's name and version, not which model is behind it.

## What others do

Each quotation was read at its source on 2026-11-13 (verified), but where a line says otherwise.

- **FHIR R4 Provenance.** One `Provenance` per activity, its `agent`s each with a `type`, "The participation the agent
  had with respect to the activity", bound (extensible) to the provenance participant types, a `who` and an
  `onBehalfOf`, "The individual, device, or organization for whom the change was made".
- **HL7 provenance participant types** (THO 2.0.0). Among them `author`, "An agent that originates, or significant
  changes to, the entity and therefore has responsibility for the information given in the entity"; `verifier`, "An
  agent who verifies the correctness and appropriateness of activity"; `enterer`, "An agent entering the data into the
  originating system, such as a scribe or transcriptionist"; `informant`, "An agent who reported information that
  contributed to the entity"; `composer`, "A device used by an author to record new information, which may also be used
  by the author to select existing information for aggregation with newly recorded information for the purpose of
  generating a new entity". The code system defines codes and no concept IRIs, as `cascade:AuthorRole` notes.
- **HL7 AI Transparency on FHIR** (1.0.0). Any resource AI produced or changed carries the security label `AIAST`,
  which "indicates that AI was involved in producing or manipulating the data or information" and "does not provide
  contextual indications, like for example if a clinician was involved in the use of the AI, or reviewed the output of
  the AI". Provenance is optional; where kept, its AI agent is a `Device`, and its examples give the AI the type
  `author` and the human who reviewed it `verifier`: a prior-authorization determination "drafted by AI", which "a
  human utilization reviewer verifies before release".
- **PROV-O.** An association "is an assignment of responsibility to an agent for an activity, indicating that the
  agent had a role in the activity"; a role "is the function of an entity or agent with respect to an activity";
  `prov:wasAssociatedWith` is the property chain `prov:qualifiedAssociation`/`prov:agent`; "Attribution is the
  ascribing of an entity to an agent", and an entity attributed to an agent "was generated by some unspecified activity
  that in turn was associated to agent ag". A software agent "is running software". PROV-O defines no roles.
- **PAV 2.3.** `pav:createdWith` is "The software/tool used by the creator (pav:createdBy) when making the digital
  resource, for instance a word processor or an annotation tool"; each PAV property is a statement on the resource
  made, not on an act.
- **MCP elicitation** (`@modelcontextprotocol/server` 2.3.1, the SDK the reference runtime uses): a server asks the
  person through the client, and the answer's `action` is `accept`, `decline` or `cancel`; a client that can show one
  declares the `elicitation` capability. The server learns the client's `clientInfo`, its name and version, and nothing
  of the model.
- **openEHR, C-CDA.** Neither records software proposing an entry that the patient then confirms (inferred, not read
  for this note).

Sources: FHIR R4 Provenance (https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/provenance-definitions.html), HL7 provenance participant types
(https://terminology.hl7.org/CodeSystem-provenance-participant-type.html), AI Transparency on FHIR
(https://build.fhir.org/ig/HL7/aitransparency-ig/, its `requirements.md`, `use_cases.md` and `EX_Provenance.fsh` at
https://github.com/HL7/aitransparency-ig on main), PROV-O (https://www.w3.org/ns/prov-o), PAV (http://purl.org/pav/), the MCP SDK in
cascade-runtime-js on 2026-11-13; the comments on jayostis/cascade-runtime-js#758.

## Choice

| thing | prior art | what we do | borrowed or diverged |
|---|---|---|---|
| where it is said | PROV-DM association with a role; FHIR's one `Provenance` per activity | on the act that wrote it, as a document's parts are (`republishing-sources.md`): an entry's session, the blank-node act a judgment's `prov:wasGeneratedBy` names, or a retraction, itself an act (`Y3`) | borrowed from PROV-DM |
| the model | FHIR `Device`; PROV `prov:SoftwareAgent`; the AI Transparency IG's AI agent | one `prov:qualifiedAssociation` of the act, in `cascade:ModelProposerRole`, whose `prov:agent` is the model, a `prov:SoftwareAgent` named by an IRI, typed in the act's file, never the runtime (`Y3`; `cascade:ModelProposerAssociationShape`) | borrowed: an agent in a role; the role is ours |
| the person who said yes | HL7 `verifier`; FHIR `onBehalfOf` | the act's association in `cascade:PatientRole`, as the person's own acts carry, whose agent is the pod's owner; an act a model proposed has one (`Y3`; `cascade:ModelProposedActShape`) | borrowed: the role the person's own acts already name |
| a judgment's author | HL7 `author` "therefore has responsibility"; PROV attribution | the person who said yes: a judgment a model proposed is `prov:wasAttributedTo` them, never the model, and is a person's judgment in every rule and view; a retraction is `prov:wasAssociatedWith` them (`Y3`; `cascade:ModelProposedJudgmentShape`, `cascade:PersonJudgmentShape`) | diverged from the AI Transparency IG, which makes the AI the author |
| told apart | the IG's `AIAST` label | a write the person made directly names no association in `cascade:ModelProposerRole`; one a model proposed names exactly one, so a reader asks for the role alone (`Y3`) | ours: the role is the label |
| when | | the act's time is the yes's: the step's, as a person's own act carries its own (`Y3`) | ours |
| asking | MCP elicitation | the door asks once per call, and writes on a yes only; a no, a dismissal and no answer write nothing (`Y2`, whose examples this note adds) | borrowed from `consent-tiers.md` |

## Why

**Why one new term, and not an HL7 code or none.** (rows "the model", "told apart") A role is what PROV-DM gives for
the part an agent played, and the shapes and every reader have to pick the model's association out by it, so it
needs an IRI. PROV-O defines no roles. The HL7 participant types are the community's list, and none fits: `author`
carries the responsibility for the content, which here is the person's, who said yes; `enterer` is a scribe, who
types what the author says, while a model also proposes judgments the person did not state; `informant` reports
information that contributed, but says nothing of a proposal the person accepted, and is used of a next of kin;
`composer` is a device the author drives. And the codes have no IRIs, so any of them would be a `cascade:` term in any
case, as `cascade:AuthorRole` is. Leaving the role off, and telling the model apart by its type alone, fails because
every act is already associated with a `prov:SoftwareAgent`, the runtime that performed it, and PROV-O makes the
qualified and the unqualified association one, so the type does not say which agent proposed.

**Why the person stays the author.** (row "a judgment's author") The rules that read judgments, which revision is
current, which records are one, whether a judgment counts, all turn on whether a judgment is a person's or the
matcher's. A judgment the person said yes to is one they chose to make: if it were attributed to the model, a Before
or a Different the person confirmed would stop counting as theirs, and every view would need a third kind of maker.
PROV's attribution is the ascribing of an entity to an agent, and the person is the one it is ascribed to; the
model's part is in the association, which PROV allows any number of. The AI Transparency IG makes the AI the author
because it records a clinical system's use of AI, where the AI's output is the product; in a pod, the person's yes is.

**Why on the act, as the person's role already is.** (row "where it is said") A person's judgment names its author's
role on the act that generated it, and a document names its author, custodian and transmitter on the act that wrote it
(`judgments.md`, row "maker"; `republishing-sources.md`). The model's part is one more association on the same act, so
a query that reads `prov:wasGeneratedBy/prov:qualifiedAssociation` reads it with no new path, and one that reads
`prov:wasAttributedTo` is unchanged. PROV-O's own property chain makes each qualified association a
`prov:wasAssociatedWith`, so the unqualified statement is not written twice.

**Why the person who said yes is the pod's owner.** (row "the person who said yes") The door acts for the person whose
pod it opened, and the question is shown to them. The role the person's own writes carry, `cascade:PatientRole`, is
the same act seen from the same side: the person acting for themselves. A separate verifier role would say no more,
and would make a confirmed judgment differ from a direct one in the person's role as well as the model's.

**Why the door names the model and what it names it by is the door's.** (row "the model") An MCP server knows the
client's name and version, not the model; a page's WebMCP knows less. So the vocabulary requires only that the model
is a `prov:SoftwareAgent` named by an IRI, typed where the act is, and never the runtime, which every act already
names by `prov:wasAssociatedWith`. A door may label it with what it knows.

## Alternatives that lost

- **The model as author, the person as verifier, as the AI Transparency IG does.** The strongest case: it is the HL7
  pattern made for exactly AI drafted, human reviewed. It lost because in a pod the author decides how a judgment
  counts, and the person's yes is what makes it count; and because `cascade:AuthorRole` already means, on a document's
  act, a party the document is attributed to, so a model in it would be attributed the judgment too.
- **`pav:createdWith` the model.** The strongest case: PAV is preferred where it has a term, and "the software/tool used
  by the creator" is close. It lost because PAV's property sits on the entity, not on the act, so a retraction, which is
  an act, has nowhere to carry it; and because a tool used by the creator is not a model proposing to them.
- **A label, as `AIAST`, on the version or the judgment.** The strongest case: one triple, always present. It lost
  because content is on a version and says what the source said, never how the pod came to hold it, and because a
  label does not say who said yes.
- **A new kind of judgment, a model's.** The strongest case: a reader could filter by class. It lost because each rule
  that reads judgments would have to name the new class, and the judgment is the person's.

## Still open

- **Naming the model across sessions.** Lean: each door names the model it heard from by one IRI for as long as it
  knows it is the same, and labels it with the client's name and version; a stable name across doors waits for a door
  that is told which model it is.
- **Recording what the person was shown.** The question the door asked could be kept as a plan of the association
  (`prov:hadPlan`). Lean: no, until a person asks why they said yes to something.

## What would change our mind

- A door told reliably which model proposed a write: a naming rule for models would follow.
- An HL7 or W3C role for an agent that proposes what a person then accepts, with an IRI: `cascade:ModelProposerRole`
  would defer to it.
- A reader who needs a confirmed proposal to count apart from the person's own: a person's judgment would then split
  by its proposer, and every rule would say which it reads.
